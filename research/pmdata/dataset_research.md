## Published provenance and methods

PMData is naturalistic sports and daily-life logging, combining Fitbit Versa 2 wrist-worn estimates, PMSys/PM Reporter Pro subjective reports and Google Forms. Participants were encouraged to wear the watch frequently, including during sleep. Default sleep tracking was used; automatic exercise recognition covered activities over 15 minutes, and participants could initiate exercise tracking manually. Thus exercise records mix logging methods, and absence of a logged session does not establish inactivity.

The nominal collection period is November 2019 through March 2020, five months and 152 calendar days. The paper states 16 participants with varied sporting backgrounds and reports 12 men and 3 women, ages 25-60, average 34. The sex counts sum to 15, so they are internally inconsistent with the stated sample size. These paper numbers must not override the actual workbook. The paper describes participant consent and a Norwegian Centre for Research Data review. This is provenance reporting, not a new legal or ethics determination for this project.

PMSys requested daily wellness, per-session training load and weekly injury reports. Google Forms requested daily meal categories, fluid glasses, body weight and alcohol consumption; smartphone reminders encouraged adherence. Food photography was requested for participants p01/p03/p05 during February-March 2020. The original paper reports 644 photos, but the audit independently counts the supplied files.

## Raw metadata findings

The workbook contains 16 participant rows, p01-p16. Raw gender text includes an encoding artifact in p06; normalizing that artifact would give 13 men and 3 women. Retain the original strings in the source inventory. Raw ages include 23 and 60, unlike the paper's lower bound of 25. The workbook's two-row header must be read with `header=1`; the first row groups a 5-km test and stride fields.

The workbook also includes height, A/B-person classification, maximal heart rate, a 5-km test date/time, and Fitbit walk/run strides. Some maximal heart rates and strides are missing. p11/p14 have only a month string for the run date; p15 is marked injured; p16 has a 2020-11-16 run date outside the nominal study period; p14's run stride is 11.3 against other much larger values. These are review flags; do not silently repair dates, infer a psychological diagnosis from A/B labels, or use later physical-test results as baseline features. The workbook-derived numeric summaries are in the executed notebook.

## Modalities and frequency

| Source | Fields / meaning | Nominal granularity and units | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fitbit heart rate | BPM and confidence | Irregular seconds; BPM | Optical estimates, no RR intervals; cannot derive validated RMSSD/HRV |
| Fitbit resting HR | Nested daily value, error field | Daily; BPM | Summary date is not availability; error semantics not fully documented |
| Steps, distance, calories | Movement/energy estimates | Minute; steps, centimeters, estimated kcal | Exported zeros may represent non-wear; do not assume distance is meters |
| Sedentary/light/moderate/very active minutes | Daily activity-level totals | Minutes/day | Whole-day values must be lagged at a 20:00 cutoff |
| HR zones | Minutes in nested zones | Daily minutes | Device-derived thresholds, not direct physiological stress measurements |
| Exercise | Type, start, durations, HR, zones, steps, optional distance/GPS | Per episode; duration milliseconds; other units source-specific | Variable schema; automated/manual logs and retrospective modification |
| Sleep JSON | Start/end, main flag, asleep/awake/bed minutes, stage arrays | Per episode; duration milliseconds; stages 30/60-second conventions | Consumer estimates; distinguish stage/classic logs and secondary episodes |
| Sleep score CSV | Overall score, components, deep minutes, resting HR, restlessness | Per scored sleep; overall 0-100 | Join with log ID; not sleep duration or a clinical cognition scale |
| PMSys wellness | Fatigue, mood, readiness, stress, soreness, sleep quality/hours | Per subjective report, usually daily | Reporting gaps, repeated reports and effective timestamps; no confirmed upload time |
| PMSys sRPE | Session end, activity list, exertion, duration_min | Per session; minutes; RPE scale convention requires range audit | Training load = perceived_exertion x duration_min |
| PMSys injury | Body locations and severity | Report / approximately weekly | Codes require a dedicated codebook; excluded from MVP |
| Google reporting | Event date, submission time, meals, weight, fluid, alcohol | Per report; kg and glass counts | Event date differs from submission; glass volume unknown; date errors/late reports |
| Food images | JPEG and optional EXIF | Per captured meal/item | Not used for numeric modeling; capture time may be missing/unzoned |

Every detected scalar/object leaf is listed in `research/data_dictionary.csv`. Nested array-element schema is separately enumerated in `reports/nested_array_schema.csv`. Field names alone are not treated as proof of units; unresolved variables are explicitly excluded pending documentation.

## Subjective scale verification

The PMData paper says five wellness scales are ordered 1-5 with 3 normal and readiness 0-10. Table I of the PMSys instrument paper resolves direction:

| Variable | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 | Score 4 | Score 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fatigue | Very tired | More tired than normal | Normal | Fresh | Very fresh |
| Mood | Very bad mood | Bad mood | Normal | Good mood | Very good mood |
| Stress | Highly stressed | Somewhat stressed | Normal | Relaxed | Very relaxed |
| Soreness | Very sore | A bit sore | Normal | Feeling good | Feeling great |
| Sleep quality | Insomnia | Restless sleep | Normal | Good | Very restful |

These are questionnaire anchors, not medical diagnoses. Higher fatigue values mean greater freshness; higher stress values mean less stress. Readiness 0 means not ready to exercise, 10 means fully ready; the older PMSys text elsewhere says 1-10, so PMData's source-specific 0-10 range governs this audit. Sleep hours are self-reported rather than device TST.

## Sleep definitions and alignment evidence

Fitbit defines `minutesAsleep` as minutes asleep, `timeInBed` as bed minutes and `duration` as milliseconds. `dateOfSleep` is the date the episode ends. Verify this empirically against raw start/end times and never shift sleep solely by the record's array position. Device episode start is not necessarily physiological sleep onset; autosleep `minutesToFallAsleep` is commonly zero. The optional interval midpoint is start+(end-start)/2, which handles midnight correctly but does not account for within-episode wake distribution.

The official Fitbit documentation says `efficiency` should be ignored for stage-based logs; it supports the standard asleep/bed calculation for classic logs. Recommend excluding the raw efficiency field for staged logs and optionally deriving a transparently named asleep/bed fraction. Fitbit stages are algorithmic estimates, not PSG scoring. Device documentation also describes split/stitch behavior for interrupted sleep, reinforcing the need to audit duplicate/multiple episodes.

Raw PMSys effective timestamps carry Z, while the Fitbit sleep timestamps are naive; sleep-score timestamps can carry Z despite matching the encoded wall-clock end time. Actual local timezone, travel and upload latency cannot be recovered with confidence from the provided fields. UTC+0/+1/+2 wellness-date scenarios in the audit are assumptions tested for sensitivity, not assertions about any person's timezone. Do not use the user's current timezone as a participant timezone.

## Limitations and astronaut-health relevance

The dataset supports an MVP exploring whether prior sleep, perceived wellness and recovery/activity history forecast future *self-reported* fatigue or wearable-estimated sleep. It provides longitudinal multimodal data useful for exercising calendar joins, missingness handling, personalization and forward validation.

It contains no identified objective cognitive performance test such as PVT, no biological circadian phase marker such as dim-light melatonin onset, and no validated astronaut operational-readiness outcome. Terrestrial routines, gravity, exercise, age/sex composition, device errors, non-wear, convenience sampling, self-report bias, inconsistent adherence and seasonal/calendar changes limit external validity. Different participant-day counts do not turn 16 people into a large independent cohort. Subject IDs and habits can create optimistic random-row validation results. Missingness may depend on fatigue or behavior and is not necessarily random.

The audit recommendations are conditional on timestamp/availability decisions and sufficient held-out label support. A sleep schedule reference measures regularity/alignment, not biological phase. An elevated fatigue label is an operational proxy, not measured cognitive impairment. All numeric audit findings and eligibility counts are generated from the supplied archive, with exclusions and assumptions documented in the executable reports.
